One address. One date. A world to explore.

SPACE × TIME × SEED

Universe Atlas is a browser-based procedural explorer built with TypeScript and Three.js. Its catalogue, life histories and terrain are generated locally. The browser requests files from the website; it computes the worlds itself.

The central design is to query a world at a location and time. A visit does not need to simulate every intervening year. Stable identities connect the universe map, local star field, planetary system, surface and dated biosphere.

World state = query(seed, canonical address, absolute time, model versions)

Rendering adds camera position, visible detail and performance budgets. Those choices decide what is drawn; the deterministic query defines what exists.

main.ts connects these systems and their UI. The mathematical modules own the generated records; planet-renderer.ts and galaxy-space.ts maintain the nearby scene. Ground movement uses Rapier physics against streamed contact geometry.

What “beginning to end” means here

The timeline is navigable; its scientific detail varies. The Big Bang is an explanatory origin. Early plasma and the CMB are illustrative fields. The far future is a labelled scenario, with 10100 years as a navigation ceiling rather than a predicted finite end of the universe.

Galaxies, stars, planet stages, water availability and local life respond to time. Atlas does not calculate cosmic structure from a full gravity simulation, evolve a planet-wide food web, or solve geological history through erosion and plate tectonics. The following chapters identify implemented behavior and its current limits.

Desktop realism, in stages

Desktop is the first visual target; a separately measured Quest profile remains later work. This build includes the canopy runtime through 98556a7: it streams complete forest patches with consistent near/far ownership, begins prefetch below 5,500 m altitude, renders below 3,500 m altitude and refreshes retained terrain during date changes. RTX 5090 preview checks passed a −4,500 m → −4,475 m → −4,500 m water-date replay with 6,301 patches and 156,309 trees retained, and more than 321 m of flight and 323 m of walking. These fixtures do not establish midrange performance or a general 60 FPS result. Earlier deployed release e8a848d used sparse distant representatives. The water/date fix is documented in the terrain refresh record. The optional scene-capture antialiasing experiment remains off by default.

The default forest still uses primitive silhouettes. An experimental EZ-Tree family now occupies the same canonical forest slots and consumes the dated tree species catalogue; it remains available only through a development hook. The normal desktop planet panel now includes a surface-lighting setting for atmospheric sky fill and bounded local sun shadows. The complete realistic forest area, matching distant tree shapes, species dimensions and broader performance acceptance remain in development.
