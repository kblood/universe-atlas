04 / Terrain & streaming

A planet, sampled at your feet.

Atlas builds terrain from a continuous field on a sphere. A distant globe and a close patch can sample the same seeded world while allocating very different amounts of geometry.

Start with a direction, not an image

terrain.ts evaluates a normalized three-dimensional direction from the body centre. This avoids a longitude seam and a special height-field singularity at the poles. The body seed selects the macro-continent field, with water inventory influencing the coastline threshold.

The base field combines continental elevation, ridges and finer noise. Local relief adds hills, gullies and rocks at approximate wavelengths of 6,500 m, 1,400 m and 260 m. Land detail fades near the base sea boundary so adding fine relief does not arbitrarily replace the seeded coastline.

Terrain version 1.5.0 adds seeded regional ridges at wavelengths of 14 km, 2.3 km and 310 m, with bounded relief up to roughly 1.9 km. These physical landforms are sampled during orbit, flight and walking; they are not created by landing. Coastal lowlands retain their established shape, and the original continental field still owns canonical drainage identities.

Latitude, elevation, moisture and body type influence surface colour and material classification. Ice, volcanic and dry worlds differ from water-bearing worlds. Gas giants use banded appearance and do not expose a walkable rocky surface.

body seed + unit direction → macro elevation → local relief → physical river field → surface sample

Surface samples separate groundHeightM, waterHeightM and waterDepthM, alongside wetness, water kind, bank influence and flow. Water therefore does not replace the underlying land height.

Six faces, progressively refined

planet-tiles.ts maps six cube faces onto the sphere. Each tile has a stable key face/level/x/y and divides into four children. Its geometry uses 32 segments per edge. Matching cube-edge coordinates normalize to matching sphere directions.

planet-renderer.ts selects patches by projected importance, ground proximity, horizon visibility and camera frustum. A dedicated worker pool builds geometry and texture data. Parent patches remain visible while children are prepared; geometry morphing and edge skirts reduce visible transitions and cracks.

  1. SelectRank visible tiles and nearby contact requirements.
  2. GenerateWorkers sample terrain, materials and water geometry.
  3. InstallBound resource installation and replace ready parents.
  4. RetireEvict distant patches and dispose GPU resources.

The focused globe remains a fallback preview until streamed tiles have useful resolution. Selection retains its previous tree and refines incrementally with hysteresis, rather than rebuilding the entire tree each frame. Ground detail comes from fresh samples of the terrain field rather than indefinitely enlarging a whole-planet texture.

These are implementation limits, not measurements of guaranteed frame rate or total browser memory. Actual resident and visible counts vary with the view, byte pressure and readiness. Ground contact can demand finer geometry than visual distance alone would select.

Walking follows settled geometry

terrain-contact.ts exposes contact patches; ground-walker.ts uses a local Rapier world with the settled triangles. Preparing a landing waits for useful contact detail. Flight clearance and surface navigation use the dated surface query.

ground-material.ts supplies close sand, soil, rock and snow detail. Terrain already has procedural material detail; further material and lighting work should read consistently alongside vegetation, water and atmosphere. Material shading improves local readability but cannot stand in for actual collision geometry. Surface survey queries identify named highlands, plains, coasts and snow destinations.

Canonical river regions modify the physical ground consistently. The regional landform layer adds bounded valley walls and talus-like relief on dry land, fading at the wet edge and the region’s 3 km limit. The base terrain used to establish canonical catchments stays separate from this regional correction.

A shared local aerial-perspective approximation now applies the terrain haze treatment to plants and animals too. It uses distance, height and daylight for visual consistency; it is not a complete atmospheric scattering model, and airless surfaces disable it. The vegetation chapter describes the forest changes. Canonical canopy streaming is implemented. The desktop Surface lighting control adds sky/ground fill and one 320 m local sun-shadow region, fading out during ascent. Terrain depth shares the visible mesh’s morph and edge locks, and nearby casters remain retained outside the camera view. XR keeps its separate lighting profile. See the implementation and validation record; realistic assets, materials and full-game performance acceptance remain in progress.

Antialiasing experiment. The diagnostic HDR scene-capture MSAA option is available but off by default. Under its memory cap it lowers the sample count before reducing capture resolution below the no-MSAA baseline. It remains experimental while resolved-depth edges, foliage/cloud fringes and shimmer are evaluated.

What terrain time travel changes today

The dated planet snapshot gates whether a surface exists and whether it is walkable. Dated sea level changes the ocean boundary. The underlying seeded continents are not recomputed by a tectonic or erosion simulation as you advance billions of years.

Procedural crater generation exists with age and resurfacing parameters, but the default terrainSampler path keeps craters disabled after performance and hydrology stability problems. Opt-in crater support is not equivalent to a fully enabled dated crater history throughout the streamed game.

Current terrain is a procedural surface model. It combines stable macro geography, local relief and regional water shaping. Geological processes, unlimited ground resolution and fully simulated changing continents remain outside the current implementation.
