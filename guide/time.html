02 / The temporal universe

A journey through cosmic time.

Atlas resolves worlds at an absolute age since the Big Bang. You can seek directly, move between eras, or let the selected date advance while the scene follows its modeled history.

The background model tabulates homogeneous radiation, matter and constant dark energy. It provides expansion, temperature approximations and particle-horizon information. CMB fluctuations and primordial structure are procedural illustrations, rather than a reconstruction of measured sky data.

Galaxies, stars and planets have histories

galaxy-history.ts defines birth, quenching and merger records. A merged galaxy resolves through its successor, with guarded merge-chain traversal. primordial-population.ts and stellar-cohorts.ts supply first stars, extinct historical populations and a smaller post-reference population.

Lineage links enforce causal birth order and use an enrichment proxy. They do not derive the resolved catalogue’s planets from simulated gas collapse or chemical evolution. First-star visits are illustrative; historical cohort visits do not contain resolved planetary systems.

stellar-evolution.ts turns a star’s seeded properties into formation, giant and remnant stages, with white-dwarf, neutron-star or black-hole outcomes. Planets progress through unformed, accreting, molten, solid and, where modeled, engulfed stages. Moons receive parent-planet context.

  1. Unformed / accretingNo queryable walking surface.
  2. MoltenA surface can be shown, but landing is unavailable.
  3. SolidEnvironment, water and local life gates determine what is available.
  4. EngulfedThe body loses its surface; navigation falls back to the system.

These stages share one planetSnapshotAt result for the inspector, surface query, ocean adapter and life gate. Following a planet retains its target across formation and engulfment, and playback pauses at major surface or existence transitions.

Using the game’s clock

Open Time, choose a cosmic era, use the position-within-era slider, or enter an absolute year as a decimal number. “Catalogue epoch” returns to the reference date; the day buttons make small local adjustments.

Most fine-control bands are linear. The black-hole-to-ceiling interval uses logarithmic navigation because it spans 60 orders of magnitude. Extreme dates are most practical to reach through checkpoints or exact entry.

Changing time while walking first leaves ground contact. Atlas keeps the nearest valid body, system, galaxy or universe view, announces the change and asks you to land again when appropriate.

State at date, observed sky and alternate fates

State at date shows a simultaneous-date catalogue. Observed sky instead estimates an emission date for galaxy glyphs using a particle horizon and a past-light-cone search, then applies redshift and approximate bolometric dimming. It is not a full spectral telescope simulation.

The default future continues constant-dark-energy expansion. Readouts retain enormous intergalactic distances logarithmically; finite rendering adapters saturate rather than allowing positions to overflow.

The Big Crunch illustration begins its transition at 80 billion years and ends at 100 billion. The Big Rip illustration transitions at 20 billion and ends at 22 billion. These are explicit chosen scenario parameters, with timeline bounds and explanatory states; they do not implement alternative Friedmann solutions or detailed disruption physics.

The ceiling is a design limit. The game offers a route from its origin to its far-future endpoint. It does not establish when, or whether, the real universe ends. Some planet histories have no modeled destruction event, and the biosphere has a separate supported age range.
