05 / Hydrology & rendering

Water below. Atmosphere above.

Atlas treats global oceans, inland waterways and sky rendering as related systems with separate responsibilities. Terrain and water queries define the surface; materials and meshes make it visible.

Rivers belong to the generated world

drainage.ts samples the base terrain, connects drainage routes and ranks catchments. hydrology-registry.ts takes the top eight canonical candidates and attempts to build a feasible river region for each. A geometrically infeasible candidate consistently yields no buildable region.

Generation runs in a worker and returns an identity-sorted canonical region set. The registry reuses results for the same body and attaches them to newly resolved body records. A user choosing “Find rivers and a lake” selects a destination among this set.

base terrain → drainage graph → ranked catchments → feasible canonical regions → carved ground + water surface

body.canonicalRivers is the physical set. body.hydrology selects the region currently receiving richer water meshes, vegetation and related rendering. Visit order and lighting can influence a suggested destination, while river existence remains independent of that suggestion.

The separate Drainage atlas is an inspection overlay: cyan routes, amber depressions and a highlighted selected main route. It reports contributing area and sampled route length, offers headwater/outlet flights and exports the drainage graph. Showing the overlay itself does not carve the terrain; the physical canonical regions supply that surface layer.

A river has a bed, banks and a surface

river-generation.ts, river-physical-field.ts and river-sample.ts provide channel and lake data, carved elevation, bank influence and flow. Terrain samples keep the bed below the water surface and preserve dry surrounding land.

river-surface.ts and its conformity and bank helpers construct visible water geometry. river-water.ts shades it, and lake-reflection.ts provides the local reflection path. The close environment module adds bank dressing appropriate to the region.

Named dry-bank destinations provide places to approach and land. Water blocks walking in the current interaction model; swimming and boats are not implemented. Tree and stone collision remains pending.

Oceans respond to the selected date

world-surface.ts resolves whether a global ocean is available using planet stage, ocean-formation date, host-star stage, catalogue water inventory and an estimated temperature band. Its broad 235–330 K suitability interval is a procedural rule, not a calibrated climate calculation.

Global sea level is interpolated from dated keys and snapped to 25 m steps for the shared renderer and surface snapshot. Tiles include the same water bucket in their work so neighbouring cached patches do not silently disagree about ocean height during a time seek.

Inland water is sampled separately. The mere arrival of an asynchronous river mesh must not decide whether a biosphere exists. Immutable water inventory and the dated environment provide those gates.

ocean-gerstner.ts provides seeded wave components with bounded time phases; ocean-water-material.ts combines them with water normals and lighting. Patch culling reduces hidden ocean work. These waves are a visual ocean model, not a fluid simulation or navigable swimming physics.

Atmosphere, clouds and rings

LayerImplementation
Atmosphereplanet-atmosphere.ts adapts a precomputed sky lookup texture and shell from sky-atmosphere.ts. Ray/shell geometry and the current sun direction support the orbital limb, haze and day/night transition.
Cloudsplanet-clouds.ts builds a procedural shell and texture; the planet renderer updates its presentation at bounded intervals.
Ringsplanet-rings.ts supplies seeded bands and gaps, a ring mesh and dated activation, integrated by the planet renderer.
Ground hazeHeight-dependent aerial appearance helps nearby terrain merge into the atmospheric horizon.

Atmosphere suitability currently relies on body heuristics, including water and gas-giant type, because the catalogue does not carry a complete atmospheric composition model. Clouds do not imply a simulated water cycle. Rings do not calculate full particle dynamics.

Rendering and physical availability are different layers. The sky and water effects communicate a world’s appearance. They do not establish weather, hydrological erosion, ocean circulation or a complete radiation-transport solution.
