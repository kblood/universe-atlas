06 / Ecology & procedural creatures

Life with a place and a history.

A world’s species history is seeded by its identity. The selected date and local conditions determine which species and habitats can be presented, from broad biosphere stages to nearby vegetation and herds.

A dated biosphere

biosphere-model.ts generates a body-keyed species history with traits, predecessor relationships, habitats and existence intervals. biosphereStateAt connects that history to the host star and planet lifecycle.

Unformed and accreting bodies have no living surface. Molten bodies report a molten stage. A solid world still needs suitable stellar conditions, water inventory and temperature before local life is exposed. An unsuitable date can temporarily report a sterile state while preserving the underlying species history.

Giant and remnant hosts, or engulfed worlds, report extinction in the current model. Beyond 20 billion years of local planet age, the runtime applies a labelled procedural extinction policy rather than carrying the last available species indefinitely.

Evolution here is a generated history. Atlas has dated species, habitat membership and lineage relationships. It does not simulate a global food web, natural selection from individual organisms, migration, or a complete planetary population.

From climate hints to local communities

surface-biomes.ts classifies surface conditions into material and biome weights. biome-ecology.ts and the biosphere model combine environmental suitability with species traits and habitat membership.

Community placements and specimen variants let the labs inspect how related organisms differ across habitats or life stages. Runtime life uses the dated snapshot, so the existence of a loaded river region or a previous camera visit cannot create the species history.

  1. IdentitySeed and body address define the species history.
  2. DateLifecycle and existence intervals select living species.
  3. HabitatTerrain and suitability determine local presentation.
  4. DetailNearby plants and animals receive geometry and animation.

Vegetation near the observer

flora-field.ts defines deterministic tree, shrub, grass and flower choices from identity, location, biome and biosphere gates. Procedural geometry includes a branching tree-growth approach and multiple detail levels.

The field can be queried across a body. The renderer streams forests during descent and evaluates ground cover and wildlife below 350 m clearance, as well as while walking. Landing does not enable life. Habitat and dated species gates apply equally to inland terrain and river regions. It does not retain a mesh for every plant on the planet.

planet-population.ts assigns fixed cubemap cells to trees. Trees are 10–22 m tall, with 18 m candidate spacing corrected for surface area. Habitat, wetness and slope determine which candidates survive. Workers select a bounded working set; habitat results are cached for up to 24,000 trees. Nearby forest is shown to 650 m, with a stable 6.5% subset of the same canonical trees selected out to 4.5 km; actual drawing ends at 4 km. All forest fades at 3–3.5 km altitude. Position and size stay fixed during approach. Coverage fades and detail hysteresis soften transitions. Distant coverage is a sparse approximation, not a complete canopy simulation.

flora-culling.ts uses packed candidate data, frustum culling and instanced detail buckets. Nearby plants get more geometry; farther plants get cheaper forms. This distinction keeps the world query separate from the number of plants the camera can afford to draw.

Current forest and flora foundations: dense forest selection follows the observer’s ground region rather than view heading. Each batch maps resident cell IDs to stable slots, using swap-removal when cells leave; this avoids rewriting a whole batch when demand order changes. Render origins and per-slot instance data are retained, with only changed attribute ranges uploaded. Water-habitat refreshes preserve resident trees while updating and hide newly submerged roots. Worker work is serialized and stale async replies are guarded. A separate flora decision cache retains the existing deterministic placement and habitat decisions while evaluating their date-dependent growth. It is not the future species asset or structural geometry cache.

Canonical trees are now grouped into bounded patches of up to 8 × 8 address cells on each cube face. Patches retain the existing population addresses and placement rules; the shared habitat check supports both near placement and patch census. Tests cover address equivalence, complete face partitioning and habitat agreement. This is groundwork only: patch census does not yet create visible canopy geometry, so distant coverage remains the same sparse representative population.

The selection worker now sends cells as one bounded transferable buffer rather than cloning one JavaScript object per cell. Canonical numeric values and order are preserved, and invalid replies fall back through the existing recovery path. For an 18,019-cell Node fixture, response round-trip p50/p95 measured 23.33/32.70 ms for object cloning and 13.79/19.42 ms for packed transfer including encode/decode. Selection time is excluded; these Node worker-thread results do not measure browser frame time or GPU performance. Browser validation and an independent review are pending. This protocol changes data transfer, not the visible forest. See the packed selection transfer record.

A shared local aerial-perspective approximation now applies terrain’s height, daylight and distance haze treatment to forest, ground plants and animals. It improves consistency across those surfaces but is not a full atmosphere model. The renderer also exposes write, upload and update diagnostics; see the desktop profiling guide for available CPU/GPU counters and their limits. A fixed-fixture comparison shows lower CPU update cost after retaining flora decisions; it does not establish a general frame-rate target.

A species asset contract now exists; forest integration is future work. It consumes the existing dated, habitat-eligible biosphere species rather than adding a second species catalogue. Its binding maps stable canonical cells to eligible species; recipes describe proportions, branching, leaves, colours and up to eight bounded structural variants. Bark and leaf tints and wind phase can vary per instance; leaf dimensions stay species-sized. The current rendered forest still uses its earlier primitive silhouettes, and the new recipes are not wired into it.

The first temperate tree asset brief passes brief validation. A revised candidate uses four original 13-leaf sprays with alpha-masked foliage and has 11,100 triangles; its GLB checks and six-view technical review pass. The foliage still looks pale, and bark/leaf materials need more work. LOD1/2, collision, runtime wind, packaging and in-game acceptance are unfinished. Fewer triangles do not establish a runtime speedup because cutout leaves can add alpha overdraw. The prototype is unshipped and the forest species recipes are not integrated. See the species and asset contract, existing flora decision-cache record and candidate review.

Production tree components, structural mesh resource eviction, full asset acceptance, integrated species geometry, coherent near/mid/far canopy, realistic lighting and shadows remain future work. Canonical patches and shared habitat verification · Opus 5.5 canopy review · Luna’s RTX 5090 renderer checks.

Animals that can be revisited in time

Living animal species and their genomes feed fauna-runtime.ts. Integer cubemap cells in surface-cell.ts give herds stable anchors and identities. A direct time seek resolves herd member poses from absolute time and bounded modular periods.

The planetary renderer draws the same genome-derived anatomy at every distance: torso, head, limbs, tail and species features are instanced in four geometry batches. Up to 128 visible individuals are selected nearest first within 600 m. Their identities survive movement between retained surface cells; the separate creature lab retains its richer rigs.

Animals roam at 0.45–0.9 m/s during their deterministic walking intervals and stand still during idle intervals. A cached terrain stencil grounds their bodies. This does not yet include obstacle avoidance or terrain-aware foot placement. Normal exploration advances universe time at 1×; pausing freezes movement and returning to a date reconstructs the same poses.
