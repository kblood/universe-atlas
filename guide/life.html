06 / Ecology & procedural creatures

Life with a place and a history.

A world’s species history is seeded by its identity. The selected date and local conditions determine which species and habitats can be presented, from broad biosphere stages to nearby vegetation and herds.

A dated biosphere

biosphere-model.ts generates a body-keyed species history with traits, predecessor relationships, habitats and existence intervals. biosphereStateAt connects that history to the host star and planet lifecycle.

Unformed and accreting bodies have no living surface. Molten bodies report a molten stage. A solid world still needs suitable stellar conditions, water inventory and temperature before local life is exposed. An unsuitable date can temporarily report a sterile state while preserving the underlying species history.

Giant and remnant hosts, or engulfed worlds, report extinction in the current model. Beyond 20 billion years of local planet age, the runtime applies a labelled procedural extinction policy rather than carrying the last available species indefinitely.

Evolution here is a generated history. Atlas has dated species, habitat membership and lineage relationships. It does not simulate a global food web, natural selection from individual organisms, migration, or a complete planetary population.

From climate hints to local communities

surface-biomes.ts classifies surface conditions into material and biome weights. biome-ecology.ts and the biosphere model combine environmental suitability with species traits and habitat membership.

Community placements and specimen variants let the labs inspect how related organisms differ across habitats or life stages. Runtime life uses the dated snapshot, so the existence of a loaded river region or a previous camera visit cannot create the species history.

  1. IdentitySeed and body address define the species history.
  2. DateLifecycle and existence intervals select living species.
  3. HabitatTerrain and suitability determine local presentation.
  4. DetailNearby plants and animals receive geometry and animation.

Vegetation near the observer

flora-field.ts selects deterministic trees, shrubs, grasses and flowers from world identity, location, biome and dated species gates. The renderer streams vegetation during descent and walking; it does not create life when the player lands or keep a mesh for every plant on the planet.

planet-population.ts gives trees stable cubemap addresses, while habitat, wetness and slope determine which candidates grow. The current game renders continuous canonical forest patches with stable tree positions as the observer moves. flora-culling.ts groups visible plants into instanced detail buckets and softens coverage and detail transitions during approach. The forest still uses legacy faceted trees; realistic authored trees are not connected to the game.

Tree rendering is under comparison in separate development labs. Static tests show substantial foliage loss as a new textured tree becomes smaller on screen. HASH preserves more small-footprint alpha coverage than MASK in one isolated sample, but it also varies more with small camera turns and a moving wind date than the downsampled comparison image. The reference is not proven ground truth, and Atlas has no general temporal antialiasing in this path. Fixed-date replay and shadow-material propagation pass in these fixtures; lighting and transitions still need work before game integration. See the Three.ez comparison, Atlas-material tree candidate, foliage filtering tests, MASK/HASH temporal results, corrected isolated coverage results, and leaf-lighting prototype.

Implementation history, experiments and measurements

Tree authoring and capture studies: V7 corrected the tree textures' linear-to-sRGB byte encoding while preserving v6 geometry, alpha and normal maps. Export, repeat checks and RTX 5090 browser checks passed, but the back-lit canopy remained dark even with cast shadows disabled; this was not visual acceptance or a full-game performance result. LOD2, species binding, packaging and midrange testing remain open. See the v7 validation record, browser prototype and colour calibration.

The separate distance-capture experiment passes repeat checks for a 16 m tree, but intermediate views differ substantially from the mesh and the 22 m / 32-pixel capture loses leaf classification. It has no forest instancing, wind, shadows or mesh handoff, and does not provide an accepted far-tree representation.

A pinned EZ-Tree Oak reference comparison produced a fuller, less tiered crown than the earlier authored-family candidate, though the trees still read as cards and the foliage remained dark. Its fixture used different geometry and leaf coverage, so it did not establish a general performance result or visual acceptance. The Opus source review records the source limitations.

The related Atlas-material candidate F retains physical leaf cards, pivots and wind data in a separate lab. Canopy, lighting and LOD appearance remain under review; the asset is not integrated or deployed. See the Opus source assessment.

The longer-term species asset contract uses dated, habitat-eligible species and bounded structural variants. The flora decision cache retains placement and habitat decisions; neither supplies authored tree geometry to the renderer.

The renderer uses complete 8 × 8 canonical census patches. The shared habitat check preserves existing population addresses and placement rules. Selection prefetch begins below 5,500 m altitude; canopy rendering begins below 3,500 m altitude, with outer fade around 3,700–4,000 m and residency to 4,500 m. These are altitude thresholds; the outer fade and residency also depend on horizontal distance. The renderer retains per-cell instance slots, uploads changed attribute ranges, and refreshes water habitat without discarding visible resident trees.

The 98556a7 preview acceptance covered a date replay from −4,500 m to −4,475 m and back while retaining 6,301 patches and 156,309 trees; visible terrain had no stale water tiles. Flight and walking checks covered 321.5 m and 323.2 m without census disagreement, queued work, capacity misses or browser errors; alpha readback was 255 at MSAA 0/2/4. These are fixture results, not a general performance guarantee. See the canopy integration record, acceptance summary, date-seek report, terrain refresh record, flight and walking report, host review assessment, water interval design, Opus water review assessment and Opus implementation review.

A CPU-only census probe covered 196,306 production-selected cells within 4.5 km in one fixture and found 149,478 trees. Worker roundtrip took 2.16 s including startup; this does not measure rendered frame time. The far-tree lab compares faceted proxy trees with the existing shape; it does not test realistic authored trees. See the census design, Opus design review, implementation review, protocol report, full-radius cost probe, worker checks and proxy experiment.

A shared aerial-perspective approximation applies terrain's height, daylight and distance haze treatment to trees, ground plants and animals. This is not a full atmosphere model. Renderer diagnostics are described in the desktop profiling guide; shared aerial perspective and optional scene antialiasing are covered in the rendering stage record. The packed selection worker and its measured Node transfer comparison are documented in the transfer record and validation evidence; those measurements are not browser frame-time or GPU results.

Earlier canopy checkpoints: the initial integration measured 153,251 trees in 6,690 patches and a 19.5 ms median frame interval in one RTX 5090 fixture. The 4c776b8 follow-up passed focused checks and flight/walk acceptance; its short CPU/GPU profiles are diagnostics, not an FPS claim. The earlier e8a848d deployed build drew sparse distant representatives. These checkpoints are retained in the foundation record and renderer validation record.

The first tree asset brief validates. Earlier candidates and authoring rounds remain useful provenance: the revised candidate uses original alpha-masked foliage, while v3 and v4 established deterministic authoring and export checks but retained pale leaves and a sparse, tiered crown. V5 addressed repeatability and introduced a lower-detail mesh, but review found bark seams, weak secondary branch support, pale foliage and a hollow crown. Review records: initial Blender candidate, v3 status, v4 smoke and render comparison, v5 evidence and v5 review assessment.

The 4c776b8 renderer profile measured CPU planet-update p50/p95/p99/max at 11.5/17.3/19.2/26.9 ms and GPU pass-sum at 4.36/6.46/7.68/8.53 ms in a short fixture. The 23de0b9 packed-transfer comparison had no regression signal in one sequential preview run. Neither establishes general speedup or a 60 FPS guarantee. See the renderer validation record and the linked desktop profiling guide.

Animals that can be revisited in time

Living animal species and their genomes feed fauna-runtime.ts. Integer cubemap cells in surface-cell.ts give herds stable anchors and identities. A direct time seek resolves herd member poses from absolute time and bounded modular periods.

The planetary renderer draws the same genome-derived anatomy at every distance: torso, head, limbs, tail and species features are instanced in four geometry batches. Up to 128 visible individuals are selected nearest first within 600 m. Their identities survive movement between retained surface cells; the separate creature lab retains its richer rigs.

Animals roam at 0.45–0.9 m/s during their deterministic walking intervals and stand still during idle intervals. A cached terrain stencil grounds their bodies. This does not yet include obstacle avoidance or terrain-aware foot placement. Normal exploration advances universe time at 1×; pausing freezes movement and returning to a date reconstructs the same poses.
