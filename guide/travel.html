07 / Navigation & experience

From the galaxy map to a riverbank.

Select a destination, fly toward it, descend into orbit and prepare a landing. Atlas changes scale as you travel while preserving the catalogue identity of the places around you.

A first journey

  1. Enter a universe seed or keep the existing one. Select a galaxy and choose to explore it.
  2. Select a mapped star. Use Approach selected star or Visit this system.
  3. Select a solid planet. Approach low orbit targets roughly 30–100 km; Descend to 10 km accounts for terrain.
  4. Use Surface destinations to inspect named land sites, or Find rivers and a lake on a suitable water-bearing planet to visit a canonical bank.
  5. Choose Land and walk. Contact detail prepares before you begin ground movement. Return to flight takes off again.
  6. Open Time to inspect the same body’s history, or copy the URL to preserve the current location and date.

Travel adapts to scale

travel.ts computes system boundaries, proximity-based speed and surface clearance. view-transition.ts blends changes of scope. A system-to-galaxy departure and a galaxy-to-system arrival are navigation transitions between related frames.

Fly to selection steers toward a chosen object; Fly outward leaves the local system or galaxy. In galaxy flight, aligned stars can be approached through the reticle. Nearby star chunks stream as the observer crosses their boundaries.

planet-flight.ts uses quaternion look and great-circle transport for orbit assistance, including polar travel. Hold orbit circles the world; movement releases it. The wheel changes orbit speed.

Free flight maintains a 120 m terrain-clearance floor. The terrain-following tour approaches approximately 600 m above suitable land. Atmospheric horizon assist gently levels roll while allowing pitch and heading control; it can be switched off.

Ground contact, water and changing dates

Rapier loads when walking is needed. A local physics frame and streamed contact triangles provide terrain collision. Ocean walking remains closed, inland water stops the walker, and decorative trees or stones do not yet provide complete obstacle collision.

A time change can remove the very ground under the observer. Atlas leaves walking first, then chooses the nearest valid observation scope. A molten world remains an orbital observation; an engulfed world falls back to its system; a missing star or galaxy can require a larger-scope fallback.

Locations and dates are represented in URLs, with world-model and build identities. Older saved views may need compatibility interpretation, and changed generator versions can produce different scenery.

Addresses, bookmarks and exports

Open Seed & catalogue tools to copy the view link, enter a system address or run Verify seed. Verification regenerates catalogue and worker results, compares them and reports a digest; it is a local consistency check rather than the full cross-browser golden suite.

Bookmark keeps up to 20 recent unique view URLs in this browser’s local storage. Copy view link provides a portable address to share or save elsewhere. Browser storage can be unavailable or cleared, so a local bookmark list is not server-side persistence.

Export downloads JSON for the current catalogue context, including available body/system records, identity records, stellar histories, surface survey results and hydrology metadata. Dedicated drainage and river-region exports expose their generated data for inspection.

The JSON export’s date.offsetNs decimal string preserves the exact date. Its legacy epochSeconds number is approximate at large ages, and exports are diagnostic records rather than a complete historical-engine save format. Versioned view URLs use the richer compatibility metadata described in Determinism.

Inspecting in VR

On a supported headset browser, choose Enter VR. Controller rays select objects; the floating panel offers Explore, Back, Fit model and Exit VR. The left stick zooms and pans, the right stick rotates, and grip moves the model.

vr.ts uses Three.js WebXR with bounded paginated panel lists, a reusable panel texture, two controller rays, reduced framebuffer scale and foveation. Immersive terrain selection has its own lower ordinary draw limit.

VR is a model-inspection path. The desktop walking controls and every new temporal or terrain feature should not be assumed to have equivalent headset interaction or fresh on-device acceptance. HTTPS is required for the public WebXR experience.

Seeing the next scale before entering it

Selecting a galaxy starts preparing its catalogue. On approach, its actual stars replace the distant silhouette between 20 and 8 galaxy radii; the local galaxy frame begins at 6 radii. Leaving uses 8 radii to avoid repeated boundary crossings. The nearest galaxy can also prepare through proximity, without a selection.

Individual stars grow gradually as you approach. Galaxy points and unresolved system stars share the same soft point profile and distance-based size curve. Systems prepare within 0.4 light-years and begin at least 6,000 AU out, while their primary star is still unresolved. Planet markers fade in as their separation from their parent becomes visible. Aligned desktop flight views overlap for 1.4 seconds.

The added interior fog field and central galaxy glow sprites have been removed. These display choices preserve catalogue positions, identities and time-dependent existence. They are readability tools rather than calibrated astronomical photometry. Slow preparation can still delay detail. See the rendering correction and visual acceptance record.
